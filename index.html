Essential Question

~3 min
How can engineers use real-world data to determine whether daylighting an intersection actually makes it safer?

You will act as a transportation engineering team, using evidence — not opinion — to answer this question.

Speaker Notes

Emphasize there are no wrong predictions right now — the entire point of today is to replace gut instinct with evidence. Tell students to remember their prediction; they will revisit it at the end.

Key Concept

~6 min

What Is Daylighting?

Daylighting is the practice of removing parking near crosswalks and corners so drivers and pedestrians can see each other clearly.

Before — parked cars block the view

After — a clear sightline for everyone

Speaker Notes

Walk through the before/after diagram left to right. Explain the parked car in the "before" panel blocks the sightline (shown by the dashed line stopping short); the "after" panel shows a curb extension (bump-out/neckdown) replacing the parking spot, opening a clear sightline. Mention that NYC Local Law 66 of 2023 requires DOT to daylight at least 100 intersections per year and publish an annual progress report, and that CST surveyors are often the ones who measure curb space to plan these treatments. (Source: NYC DOT, Daylighting and Street Safety: An Analysis, Jan. 2025 — DOT's own before/after data found neckdowns nearly triple the average time between pedestrian injuries, from 1.4 to 4.7 years.)

Why It Matters

~5 min

Street Safety Is an Engineering Responsibility

Vision Zero

NYC's policy commitment to eliminate traffic deaths and serious injuries for every street user — children, older adults, cyclists, transit riders, people with disabilities, and drivers alike.

Citywide Mandate

NYC law now requires daylighting at intersections to improve pedestrian visibility.

Proven Impact

Curb extensions ("neckdowns") — the treatment shown in Slide 5 — cut severe pedestrian injuries by nearly 45%.

Source: NYC DOT, Daylighting and Street Safety: An Analysis (Jan. 2025)

Engineers carry an ethical responsibility: design decisions on paper become real outcomes on real streets.

Speaker Notes

Connect this to engineering ethics — the NSPE Code of Ethics places public safety above all else. The third card cites NYC DOT's own January 2025 daylighting study: curb & sidewalk extensions reduced pedestrian killed-or-severely-injured outcomes by 44.7% in DOT's Safety Treatment Evaluation. Keep the tone serious but not alarmist; this motivates why the data work today matters.

Critical Thinking Checkpoint

~7 min

Correlation vs. Causation

Hydrant Zones
(Accidental Daylighting)

?

30% Higher Injury Rate

Why? DOT's own data points to "risk compensation" — more visibility can make drivers feel safer, encouraging faster, less cautious driving. Other factors DOT had to rule out: population density, truck routes, travel lanes, speed, intersection control type…

NYC DOT's real 2025 study found intersections daylighted by accident (via fire hydrants) had higher injury rates — a reminder that correlation is not causation.

Speaker Notes

This is the statistical-reasoning core of the lesson, and it's a real, surprising finding — not a hypothetical. NYC DOT's January 2025 report found intersections with "hydrant zones" (fire hydrants or bus stops that accidentally clear parking near a corner) had a 30% higher normalized rate of pedestrian and total injuries than similar nearby intersections. Ask students: does removing parking CAUSE more injuries? DOT's own explanation is "risk compensation" — increased visibility can make drivers feel safer and drive faster, with less caution. Have students brainstorm other confounding variables DOT had to control for (population density, truck routes, number of travel lanes, vehicle speed, intersection control type) before moving on. Tell them they must apply this same lens when they analyze Syntra data later.

Build Your Case

Writing Your Engineering Recommendation

Claim

Evidence

Reasoning

Explain how the evidence supports your claim — and acknowledge at least 1 limitation of the available data.

Memo Template

From: [ Your Team Name ]

Re: Daylighting Expansion Recommendation

[ Claim + Evidence + Reasoning, in your own words ]
